Background

Seventeen years in professional kitchens, then a career change that was not optional.

I cooked from 2002 to 2019 — line cook to kitchen manager and executive chef, including a semester teaching culinary arts in the Hospitality and Tourism diploma at what was then GPRC, and about a year of consulting for a restaurant owner after I came off the line. What I actually did all day was run an operation: budgets, purchasing, suppliers, scheduling, human resources, and the custom Excel systems I built to keep cost, staffing, ordering and waste under control.

I left the line in 2019 because I have Hajdu-Cheney syndrome, a rare connective-tissue disorder, and kitchen work stopped being sustainable. The next few years were a couple of surgeries, a few injuries, and being the stay-at-home parent. I decided to learn programming. When AI crashed that party, I decided the better bet was to understand AI itself, and started building and shipping things in public. The useful half of the kitchen years transferred intact: decide what good means, write the standard down, and hold every plate that leaves to it.

What I do now is specify software and direct AI to build it — which is a real skill with real failure modes, and the difference between a working result and a plausible-looking one is entirely in the specifying, the reviewing and the refusing to ship. The design calls, the evidence rules and the judgement about what is good enough are mine, and so are the mistakes.
